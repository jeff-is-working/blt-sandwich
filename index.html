What does a BLT cost? Average local BLT price by state, October 2026

Verified sample (157 prices re-checked). Prices collected October 11, 2026.

Menu prices for a bacon, lettuce and tomato sandwich, taken from local restaurants' own online menus. This is a convenience sample of 650 restaurants in 50 states, not a survey, and it covers only places that post a price online.

Key numbers

  • $12.00 national median, all BLTs (n = 650). Mean $12.08.
  • $11.00 national median, classic BLTs only (n = 453). Mean $11.34.
  • $13.75 Big Front Door, San Diego (the r/food post): $1.75 above the national median, higher than about 67% of prices here and higher than about 32% of the 17 California prices.

Key judgments

What a BLT costs (moderate confidence) At local and regional restaurants that publish a price online, a BLT very likely costs about $12 nationally: median $12.00, mean $12.08. Classic BLTs only: median $11.00, mean $11.34. Among states with 10 or more restaurants, the highest means are Alaska $17.42, Hawaii $15.33, Washington $15.13; the lowest are Connecticut $9.62, Virginia $9.94, Louisiana $10.16, Kentucky $10.28, North Carolina $10.28. Do not rank states in the middle of the range, and do not rank South Dakota (fewer than 5 restaurants). Rural prices split into cheap farm-town diners and expensive resort towns, so one rural average hides both (rural median $12.00; low-to-moderate confidence).

Delivery-app markup and fees (low confidence) When a restaurant lists its BLT on a third-party menu, the item price is often, but not always, higher: 8 like-for-like pairs show a median of +17.9% (range +0.0% to +30.0%), and some restaurants charge the same price. Platform fees come on top (DoorDash service fee 15%), but delivery fees are generally not observable without an account and checkout, so they were not measured.

Is $13.75 in San Diego reasonable? (moderate confidence) For California, $13.75 is reasonable: it is higher than about 32% of the 17 California prices. Nationally it is a bit above typical, not wild: $1.75 above the national median and higher than about 67% of prices. Big Front Door is the only San Diego row, so "for San Diego" here means "for California".

One counting ruling (decision) The Golden Lion mahi mahi BLT (Florida) counts as a variant, because it is sold as a BLT and has bacon and tomato. That keeps it consistent with the salmon and crab BLTs that also count.

Fees

  • DoorDash's policy page says its service fee is 15% of the subtotal for most restaurants, plus small-order fees in some cases.
  • Grubhub showed a $1.49 delivery fee and a 15% service fee (capped at $7.50) for the Fat Shallot example.
  • Delivery fees are generally not observable without an account and checkout, which this study did not do; the few shown here (for example the Grubhub $1.49) come from public policy panels. The regular delivery fee, taxes and tip appear only at checkout. A "$0 delivery fee" banner on a delivery app is a first-order promotion, not a standing fee, so no delivery-fee average is shown.
  • Illustration only: a markup of 15 to 20% plus a 15% service fee puts a delivered BLT about a third above the restaurant's own price, before any delivery fee and tip.

About this study

Method note: how the numbers were made, and their limits

How the prices were collected

Prices were read from local restaurants' own online menus and ordering pages in October 2026. For each state we looked for independent and small regional restaurants that list a BLT with a price, in a mix of rural, small-city and urban places. Each restaurant counts once, using its plain BLT when it has one (otherwise its first listed BLT). Variants such as a BLT with avocado, a salmon BLT, or a BLT on a wrap or bagel count, and are flagged as variants. Where the same BLT appears on two of a restaurant's own pages, the live ordering price is used.

What was left out

  • National chains and large regional chains (more than about 25 locations).
  • Menus dated before 2025.
  • Bundles that include a drink or dessert, and combos or box meals.
  • Sandwiches with no bacon (for example tempeh), clubs with turkey or ham, salads and burgers.
  • A second location of the same brand in the same city.
  • Cafeterias and similar non-restaurants.
  • Prices seen only on an aggregator site. No rural secondary sources were used.

How it was checked

A sample of 157 prices (about one in five) was re-checked by opening the source page again. 143 matched (91%), 6 sources could not be reached that day, and a few were wrong or out of date. Two prices were corrected where a flat-text page had put a price next to the wrong item. The remaining prices come from the same method but were not re-checked one by one. Place sizes for several rows were re-checked against the Census address geocoder; where the geocoder could not give a population, the original size was kept.

How the seed price is placed

"Higher than about X% of prices" is a percentile rank in which a price equal to $13.75 counts as half. It is worked out against all included restaurants nationally, and against the California restaurants separately.

Limits

  • Online-menu selection. Only restaurants that post a price online are here. That probably favors newer, more urban and higher-priced places, and Toast-hosted menus are common.
  • Convenience sample. It is not random and not weighted by population or number of restaurants.
  • Small numbers. A few states have fewer than 10 restaurants; one has fewer than 5. Treat those as examples, not averages.
  • Base prices. Some menus show a starting price ("from"), which understates what a customer pays.
  • Rural coverage. Rural restaurants with an online price are mostly tourist businesses, which probably pushes rural prices up.
  • Delivery. Delivery fees are generally not observable without an account and checkout, so they are not measured; the few fees mentioned on this page come from public policy panels. Some restaurants' own ordering pages may already include app markup.
  • Time. Prices are as listed on October 11, 2026 and change often.
